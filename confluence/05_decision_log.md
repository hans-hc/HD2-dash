# Methods and decision log
The case study was developed in November 2026. This documentation was prepared for Jira and Confluence on November 9. It describes completed analysis separately from proposed game-team follow-up. No interviews or stakeholder approvals are claimed.

| Decision | Reason | Limit or reason to revisit |
|---|---|---|
| Use a retrospective comparison | The historic events and their outcomes were already available. | This was not preregistered and the sample is small. |
| Expand the original three cases to eight 2024 Warbonds | Check the timing idea against more examples, including unfavorable outcomes. | The comparison does not cover all later releases or all in-game events. |
| Use historical daily Steam peaks | The inspected export distinguishes daily rollups from recent intraday samples. | Timezone metadata is absent. The grouping is an explicitly disclosed inference. |
| Use release statements rather than publication dates | Announcements often appeared before the Warbond was due. | Most dates are scheduled availability rather than verified deployment timestamps. |
| Define a seven-day nearby patch window, including hotfixes | Make the patch-gap rule explicit and repeatable. | It does not establish intentional bundling or equal patch scope. |
| Check alternative baselines and anchors | Expose dependence on the window choice and patches preceding the Warbond. | Stable direction does not establish causation. |
| Recommend a 35-day review rather than a fixed gap | Similar gaps had mixed later outcomes. | A studio would need comparable content, costs and player-level evidence before a scheduling change. |
