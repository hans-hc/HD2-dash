# Evaluate release sequences before changing content cadence
**DRAFT wording for owner review.** Hansen selected content timing on November 5, 2026. The completed public-data extension compares eight post-launch 2024 Warbonds. The proposed production process below has not been implemented by the game team.

## Recommendation
Adopt a release-sequence review with launch, weekly and later checkpoints before changing cadence. Similar patch-to-Warbond gaps had mixed outcomes, so September's favorable result does not justify a fixed two-day schedule. Compare content scope and overlapping releases alongside timing, and retain outcomes that contradict the preferred explanation.

## What the public analysis measures
| Check | Definition | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline | Mean daily Steam peak over days −14 to −1 | Establish the previous activity level and inspect its trend. |
| Launch | Maximum daily Steam peak over days 0–3 | Describe immediate attention. A maximum is not directly comparable to a later mean as a drop-off rate. |
| Weekly | Mean daily Steam peak on days 0–6, 7–13 and 14–20 | Track activity using consistent seven-day summaries. |
| Later | Mean daily Steam peak on days 22–35 | Check whether activity remains above its previous level. |
| Sensitivity | Repeat with 7- and 28-day baselines, and re-anchor before the nearby patch cluster | Expose dependence on the baseline and event date. |

The completed extension uses the Warbond date as its primary anchor. The alternative cluster anchor is the earliest officially documented versioned patch in the preceding seven days, including the Warbond date. This rule includes hotfixes and is not a claim about a planned release bundle. Both baseline and follow-up move when the anchor changes. All eight cases have complete windows; later direction is unchanged under the checked alternatives.

## Proposed review workflow
1. Before each future review, specify eligible releases, comparison windows and known scope categories. The current study is retrospective and does not claim preregistration.
2. Record actual content availability separately from announcement dates. Attach official evidence, patch scope, adjacent releases, and any verified promotions or access changes. Preserve uncertainty when only a scheduled date is available.
3. Plot daily activity around the complete sequence. If a patch precedes the Warbond, inspect a pre-sequence baseline as well as the Warbond anchor. Report both when they materially differ.
4. Review after each complete weekly window and after day 35. Retain missing observations as unavailable; do not substitute sampled maxima or partial-window averages.
5. Write a short decision note: what persisted, what else changed, and whether a timing hypothesis deserves further testing. Compare several similar release scopes before recommending a scheduling change.

## Decision rule and limits
The present evidence supports standardizing measurement, not selecting an optimal release gap. A scheduling proposal would require a repeated association across comparable content, stable results under reasonable window choices, and an explicit account of overlapping releases, promotions and lifecycle trends. Production capacity and costs would also need review. No numeric success threshold or cadence change has been approved.

Daily concurrency does not measure unique players, individual retention, revenue, conversion or console behavior. A game team could add player cohorts, mission activity, content exposure and production costs to evaluate business value; these are optional future internal validation steps, not prerequisites falsely claimed as completed here. Monthly review share remains context and cannot isolate reactions to releases two days apart.
