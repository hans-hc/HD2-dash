# My recommendation and review plan
I recommend making a 35-day release review part of LiveOps planning, so future schedules are based on both the launch response and what happens afterward. This is a proposed process; the completed work is the public-data analysis and report.

## What I would record
For each release, record the patch and Warbond dates, what content changed, nearby updates and whether activity was already rising or falling. Compare releases with similar types and amounts of content before recommending a standard gap.

## Review checkpoints
| Check | Measure | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Before release | Average daily Steam peak on days -14 to -1, plus the daily trend | Establish the comparison level and existing direction. |
| Launch | Highest daily peak on days 0-3 | Describe the immediate response. |
| End of week 1 | Mean daily peak on days 0-6 | Check the first full week using an average. |
| End of week 2 | Mean daily peak on days 7-13 | Check whether activity remains elevated. |
| End of week 3 | Mean daily peak on days 14-20 | Track the following week consistently. |
| Day 35 | Mean daily peak on days 22-35 against the baseline | Check the later activity level. |

The completed study is anchored on each Warbond. If a patch comes first, I would also inspect a baseline before the patch sequence and show how shifting the anchor changes the result. Each metric needs every daily observation in its window. Missing days stay unavailable; sampled maxima do not replace daily peaks.

## How the review informs a decision
Four of five two-day-gap cases were below baseline later, so I would compare similar releases before recommending that spacing. Flag major overlapping updates so an increase around a new faction or balance overhaul is not credited to timing alone. A repeatable association across similar content would justify closer investigation; this sample does not establish an optimal gap.

A game team could add returning-player cohorts, mission activity, purchase behavior, technical issues and production costs before making a business decision. Those measures require internal data and are proposed follow-up work. No numerical success threshold or live schedule change was tested here.
